# Case 018: A neighbour wants a signature

## Overview

A neighbouring occupier sent Theo Glen an unsigned, undated letter. It says water from his flat damaged their ceiling and asks him to sign that he accepts liability and to promise £900 today. Theo told us he does not know where the water started, but then asked us to reply that he is at fault and that his insurance will pay. We declined. We notified Birchmere Mutual of a possible third-party claim and sent them the letter, then advised Theo. The case stays open until Theo answers our questions and Birchmere confirms.

## Next action at escalation

Done: Birchmere Mutual was emailed notice of a possible third-party claim with the neighbour's letter attached (/messages/002). We told them nothing has been admitted, shared the proposed holding wording, and asked for a claim reference, their position on liability cover and excess, whether they will deal with the neighbour directly, and what evidence they need. Theo was then emailed (/messages/003). We told him not to sign, admit fault or pay, and that the same-day deadline is the neighbour's own. We gave him neutral wording he can send today and steps to take if water is still escaping (stopcock, electrics, plumber, managing agent or landlord). We asked him five things: is it still leaking; when did the leak happen and how and when did the letter arrive; has he already said, signed or paid anything; does he own or rent, and is there a managing agent or freeholder; is his own flat damaged and has the source been found. Waiting on: Theo's answers, especially whether the leak is active and whether he has already admitted anything or paid; and Birchmere's acknowledgement, claim reference, position on cover and instructions for handling the neighbour. If Theo hasn't replied, chase him later today. If Birchmere hasn't acknowledged, chase them on the next working day. Then pass Theo's answers to Birchmere and Birchmere's instructions to Theo. Do not close until Birchmere confirms how it will handle the claim.

### 2. Email to Theo Glen: Your neighbour's letter: please don't sign or agree to pay

> We can't send that reply. Please don't sign the letter, admit fault or promise to pay anything yourself either.
> 
> Why:
> - You don't yet know where the water came from, so saying you are at fault may not be correct.
> - We can't promise Birchmere Mutual will pay. Only they can decide that, and we haven't confirmed yet what your policy covers for this.
> - Liability cover usually says you must not admit fault or offer payment without the insurer's agreement. Doing so can put your cover at risk. We've asked Birchmere to confirm what your policy says.
> - The "today" deadline is the neighbour's own. You don't have to meet it.
> 
> What we've done: today we sent Birchmere Mutual the letter as notice of a possible claim against you. We told them nothing has been admitted. We asked whether your policy covers this and how they want to deal with the neighbour. This is not yet a decision to pay.
> 
> If you want to answer the neighbour today, send only this:
> "I have received your letter. I am not able to accept liability or agree any payment. I have referred your letter to my insurer."
> Don't give them your policy details. If Birchmere wants different wording, we'll tell you.
> 
> If water is still coming through, or you're not sure:
> - Turn off the water at the stopcock if you can safely reach it.
> - Don't touch wet switches or sockets. Switch off at the fuse box only if it is dry and safe to reach. Call an electrician if water is near electrics.
> - Call a plumber to find the source. If it isn't in your flat, it may be a shared pipe. In that case, tell the managing agent or freeholder, or your landlord if you rent.
> Keep photos, the plumber's written findings and any receipts.
> 
> Please reply with:
> 1. Is water still leaking anywhere?
> 2. When did the leak happen, and when and how did you get the letter?
> 3. Have you already said, signed or paid anything to the neighbour?
> 4. Do you own or rent the flat? Is there a managing agent or freeholder?
> 5. Is your own flat damaged, and has anyone found the source yet?
