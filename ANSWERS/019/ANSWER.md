# Case 019: The contractor says it is approved

## Overview

Fictional Kitchen Works plans to start a full kitchen replacement at 3 Meadow Steps on 13 Nov and bill Tidebank. Their reason is second-hand: they say the customer, Sylvia Moss, told them someone at the insurer said yes verbally. The only thing Tidebank has put in writing on claim TB-CH-019 is that it has agreed an inspection visit, and it has asked for the damaged parts to be kept. On 12 Nov I warned Sylvia, Tidebank and the contractor. The case stays open until Tidebank confirms in writing what work is authorised.

## Next action at escalation

Done on 12 Nov. I emailed Sylvia Moss (/messages/002) explaining that Tidebank has only agreed an inspection and asking her to have the contractor hold off and keep the damaged parts. I asked who at Tidebank said yes, whether she has hired the firm or agreed a start date, and whether we may discuss her claim with them. I emailed Tidebank (/messages/003) with the contractor's note attached. I asked it to confirm in writing, before work is due to start, whether anyone approved more than the inspection (checking its call notes), whether the firm is its appointed contractor, what work is authorised, the inspection date and whether contractors can bill it directly. I sent the contractor a short holding reply (/messages/004). It says we hold no written approval and asks them not to start or throw away damaged items until the insurer confirms in writing. I recorded everything in a case note (/messages/005). Waiting on Tidebank's written answer and Sylvia's reply. When Tidebank answers, pass its answer to Sylvia as given, and to the contractor only if she agrees. Early on 13 Nov, check whether work has started. If it has, tell Tidebank straight away and ask Sylvia to photograph the damage and keep the parts. If Sylvia hasn't replied by then, phone her, provided the brokerage has a number; there isn't one in the case file.
