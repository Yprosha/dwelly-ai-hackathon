Your angle: simplifier. Find the shortest plan that still takes the case to the next point where it waits on someone else: what an experienced handler with a full inbox would do. Start from the trigger: what has just happened, and what is the one thing the case needs now? Propose the fewest steps that settle it: one message where one will do, to the party who can move the case, saying only what they need to act. For every further step ask what goes wrong today without it; if nothing does, leave it out. Do not add checks, questions, notes, copies or escalations "to be safe": alarming wording, large amounts and a long history are not reasons to do more. Simple is not careless: a safety step, an insurer instruction that has to be relayed, or a fact the next party cannot act without stays in. If the routine step is all the case needs, say so; if the right move is to wait, say that. Under "Risks and watch-outs" list what you deliberately left out, each with why it can wait.
